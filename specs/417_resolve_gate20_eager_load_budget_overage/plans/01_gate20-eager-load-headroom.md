## Overview

The dispatched premise is stale, and the plan is scoped around that fact rather than around the
description's numbers. Measured live at plan time: the eager-load total is 66,707 B against a
recorded baseline of 66,800 B, `verify-deploy.sh --only-gate 20` exits 0 with 4 of 4 checks
passing, and `test-verify-deploy-context-budget.sh` passes 15 of 15 cases. The overage the
description reports (67,088 B against 66,100 B, with 2 of 13 suite cases failing) was already
closed by commit `9a43026d2` ("orchestrate conclusion: channel (a) direct fixes", 2026-10-16),
which took a hybrid route: a 381 B relocation trim of genuinely-duplicated stash-pop guard prose,
followed by a deliberate, dated baseline move from 66,100 B to 66,800 B with its rationale
recorded inline in `orchestrator-context-budget.json`'s `eager_load.note`.

Every verification criterion in the task description is therefore already satisfied. What is NOT
satisfied is the task's stated goal, which prefers route (a) and names the durable problem as
persistent noise in every implementation plan's closure criterion. A 93 B margin is not headroom:
any ordinary addition to an eager rule or a merged `claudemd` fragment re-fires the gate
immediately, and the same noise returns. This plan therefore implements route (a) properly --
recover real, durable eager headroom by relocating duplicated eager content into existing
non-eager homes, leaving `baseline_bytes` unmoved at 66,800 B so the recovered slack becomes
headroom rather than a raised ceiling.

The route decision is recorded here explicitly, as the task demands: **route (a), trim; no
further baseline movement.** The previously-recorded baseline move stands as a reviewed decision
and is not reversed -- lowering a reviewed regression ceiling after a trim has no precedent in
this config's own history, where every trim has landed under the standing baseline with the
baseline left in place.

## Rollback/Contingency

Every phase commits its own green sub-steps, so the ordinary recovery is `git revert` of the
specific commits -- no working-tree discard is involved and no snapshot is needed. Phase 1 writes
nothing at all and has nothing to roll back. A Phase 2 relocation that turns out to have lost a
clause is fixed forward: restore the clause at its destination, which is where it belongs, rather
than reverting the trim. Phase 3 is a single JSON edit, revertible on its own. If the trim must
be abandoned entirely, reverting the Phase 2 and Phase 3 commits returns the config and content
to the already-passing 66,707 B / 66,800 B state established in Phase 1, which satisfies every
verification criterion in the task description on its own -- so abandonment is a safe terminal
state here, not a failure. Should a genuine whole-tree rollback over uncommitted work ever be
needed, follow `context/contracts/recovery.md`'s rollback rung for the exact invocation shape,
including its out-of-scope override flag; never emit a bare precautionary `git-snapshot.sh`.
