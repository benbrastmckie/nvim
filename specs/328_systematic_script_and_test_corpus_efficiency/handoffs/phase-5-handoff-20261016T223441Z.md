**Key Decisions Made**:
- Direct inspection (not the duplicate-block hash count the "four remaining wrapper-carrying
  lints" framing was staffed from) found that only `lint-scoped-commit-boundary.sh` carries the
  literal byte-identical two-pattern wrapper the lib already implemented.
  `lint-task-lookup-adoption.sh` and `lint-hook-registration-paths.sh` each carry a materially
  different, bespoke pattern-matching contract (a four-pattern extension-scoped structural
  filter; a two-group classification whose fast-path and slow-path branches use genuinely
  different, non-interchangeable pattern-matching semantics) that the current lib's
  single-pattern-set, flat-`FILES`-array contract cannot safely replicate. Both are recorded as
  reasoned exclusions (`[COMPLETED WITH EXCLUSIONS]`, full Reasoned Exclusions table in the
  plan) rather than forced through the lib at risk of a silent finding-set change.
- `lint-shellcheck.sh`'s own default enumeration is deliberately `git ls-files`-tracked, not a
  directory `find` walk (its own documented DUAL FILE-SET CONTRACT) — only its fast-path
  (shared-corpus) and explicit-path branches were migrated onto the lib; the git-ls-files middle
  branch stays lint-specific, by design, not as a deferred migration.
- `VIOLATIONS` accounting and the exit-code-convention summary/report sections were evaluated
  and NOT extracted into a shared function: each lint's summary carries distinct metric names
  and violation categories (confirmed by reading all seven files' closing sections), so a
  generic shared formatter would need as much interface-plumbing as it saves — net-neutral-to-
  positive, the opposite of this phase's mandate.
- The widened fast-path guard accepts EITHER calling convention
  (`scan_paths` empty, OR `scan_paths` exactly `[DEFAULT_SCAN]`) in one function, rather than
  forcing every caller onto one shape, per the dispatch's explicit instruction.
